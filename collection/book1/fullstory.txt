# Chapter 1

	There is this phenomenon in the world derived from human nature. The concept of ghosts, folklore, legends, tales, apparitions. Tales from centuries dating back to the medieval ages and scary rumors we tell during high school. A bedtime story our parents would tell us when we were young and superstitions about ghost towns or lone roads. Superficially arbitrary to someone yet all the same to me.
An old Japanese legend about the great Nine-Tailed Fox, a Chinese folklore about a fox spirit, a superstition about a trickster fox woman, a rumor about a glistening, black haired woman lying bloodied on a pavement wall.
And to me, a curse carved deep into my soul. A reminder that stories should not be taken lightly. A story of my humanity written in dried black ink and catalogued as its own legend. A story that I will continue to keep for myself.

# Chapter 2

	It was May 15th, four days until summer ended and I returned to high school. Five days until the orientation week officially starts, which requires all students to attend an opening ceremony. To be honest, I was thinking about skipping out entirely. As an upcoming senior, I would feel more woeful about my future responsibilities if I attended orientation. The thought of that made me irritated at my pettiness. While other students had already made plans for their future university, I was strolling across the road on my bike doing errands for someone else.
	“Hey! Wait up Luca!”
	I halted immediately and glanced around my shoulder to see a feminine figure closing the distance on me.
	“You know… It’s dangerous going that fast on your bike.” She takes a few more breaths before continuing. “Had you gone any faster, you might have missed me entirely.”
	In front of me stood Jasmine Verity, a student whose intellect rivals that of teachers. She stood at the top of the school food chain. To be more specific, she held the highest grades of all students and has yet to make any errors on her exam. For some reason, a grandiose person like her veered her attention towards me. Initially, we had conversed around last spring break when my life became a living hell. But even before those events, Jasmine Verity befriended Luca Caecus. To my dismay, she disregarded the opinions expressed on her reputation.
	 “The speed limit around here is 20 miles per hour. You managed to just go under 20 on your bike.” Jasmine was clearly reprimanding me. “It would really be a hassle for me if you somehow got a speeding ticket.”
	“Sorry about that, I guess I wasn’t paying much attention.”
Jasmine looked unconvinced. “Well then, you wouldn’t leave a young girl unattended now, will you?”
	“Alright, I got it.” I answered as I got off my bike.
	“Luca.”
	“Yes?” I hesitantly said.
	“You’re coming to orientation, right? Your attendance is at upmost importance considering your vice president status.”
	“Uh, the thing is, I have a lot of things I have to attend first which incidentally cuts into the entirety of orientation week-.”
	“Mr. Caecus?”
	Whoa, scary! It’s one thing to be called by my last name by my mom but it feels totally different when someone else does that.
	“Yes ma’am, I had no intention of skipping.”
	“That’s good to hear. It would be a bad look for me if my vice president wasn’t there when I give my ceremony speech.”
	“Whose fault do you think that is?”
	I can’t say that I’m a delinquent or troublemaker. While I don’t find myself causing issues outside of school, I do make attempts to displace myself away from my classmates.  But after spring break, Jasmine had full intentions of straightening me out to get me on track academically. During the end of spring break, Jasmine accounted me as vice president. The decision was an abrupt call for most since Jasmine, who could manage the student council all on her own, decided to appoint a “nobody”. Despite my attempts to distance myself from school, Jasmine pulled me back with an iron grip. But because I had owed her so much for the events during spring break, I had no reason to deny it. Instead, I was a bit relieved.
	“I was meaning to ask, is there something clouding your mind?” Jasmine asked as she covered the side of her cheek.
	“Oh…, I was just thinking how much I hate summer.” I answered quickly.
	“Really? A few weeks back you were claiming how summer was the best season to catch up with all the games you piled up-.”
	“I had a change of heart! Wait hold on, that’s some extraordinary memory you have. I can’t believe you remembered something so trivial.”
	More than that, what kinds of thing does she remember me saying? I feel like any opinion I express might become a landmine.
	“Look, I’m not to fond of the dry and humid temperature, and I hate the wet feeling of sweaty clothes.”
	“Not that you go outside anyways…” She sneaks in.
	“Anyways! Not to mention, all the bugs become active during summer.”
	“That’s true, a third of this town is just forest after all.”
	“Really? I hadn’t noticed something like that. I don’t think I’ve seen any forest around here.”
	“That’s because our town’s zoning regulations condenses buildings towards one area and leaves the rest unattended. I’d say you would have to travel 7 miles north of here to reach a forest.”
	“You sure do know a lot of trivial things.”
	“Well, I’ll lay another one on you.”
	“Please don’t.”
	Completely ignoring me, she begins, “In a recent community ecology study, this town has 42 different invasive insect species.”
	“Huh? That’s way too many! What does that mean for the environment?”
	“I’m not sure about that, but you can’t really blame them for being here.”
	“How do you mean?”
	“Think about it Luca, most of the invasive species didn’t come here by choice. They were introduced to here by us.”
	For a second Jasmine’s head drooped, and I noticed again the weird marking on her cheek. She caught me glaring and suddenly snapped back.
	“Keep your eyes to yourself weirdo.”
	I found myself tangled by her words. “I-I wasn’t looking at anything indecent!”
	“Ahaha. I know, I’m just joking.” She laughs in my face before changing her tone again. “But it’s pretty impressive you were able to notice something like that.”
	The words suddenly left my mouth without me realizing. “Are you hurt there?”
	She reluctantly reaches for her cheek and brushes away what I presume to be makeup. She then slowly peels away a bandage that hid her bruise. Surprisingly, she held a smile all while showing me her injury.
	I have to mention the extraordinary nature of Jasmine Verity. Our meet during spring break was no mere “accident”. What transpired was a collection of misery and pain I rather keep hidden. But Jasmine wasn’t normal. She had stayed through these events despite putting her life on the line and did it all while keeping a smile. An image like that felt more scarier than the things that happened to me over the break.
	Initially, I chose to ignore that fact and accepted Jasmine for what she was: A saint whom I owed my life to. But today that impression felt jeopardized. Because the injury on her face was no accident. It could only exist from malicious intent. I felt irritated knowing someone had the intent to hurt her.
	“Can I see that injury on your face?” I firmly ask.
	“You know, even when I try to swerve attention away from it, you still manage to notice.”
	“Of course I would! If I saw one of my friends hurt, I would obviously ask to help. Are you an idiot?”
	She pauses at my comment for a moment, “Possibly.”
	“So, what happened?”
	“Before that Luca, promise me you won’t tell anyone.”
	I reluctantly agreed.
	“My father got into an argument with me. One thing after another…, he slapped me”
	Judging by her injury, that slap was done so forcefully that she bled from her cheek.
	“Jasmine…, I can’t excuse that kind of abuse.”
	She desperately held onto my arms. “You must! It was just a misunderstanding. It’s my fault my father hit me.”
	The anxious feeling in my heart screamed out. I really wanted to refuse. I wanted to confront the man and say everything I had pent up. But I owed Jasmine too much. My burden silenced me, and I agreed to tell no one.
	“My name isn’t really mine. My last name, Verity, comes from my foster parents who adopted me around six. I’ve been with them for twelve years, more time than I spent with my original family. I was named after a flower that grew across the valley somewhere in Southeast Asia. But after my family passed, I moved in with my foster family. We relocated a few times and eventually settled here in this rural town. After a few years with my foster parents, I found out that their late daughter passed away around the same age they adopted me. During the first year with my foster parents, I felt the most alone in my life. I felt that fate took away my parents. So, I began working away the pain. I studied until my eyes dried and wrote until the skin of my hand peeled off. I swore to never rely on fate and keep living to the truest intentions. But my foster parents didn’t get along with me too well and I don’t blame them. I reminded them of everything wrong with themselves with my preachy personality. I represented myself with only righteous values. Though, I had failed to realize how mentally taxing it was for my foster parents.”
	“Even so! That gives them no right to abuse you like that. Not ever.”
	“It’s all right Luca. What’s done was done.”
	I stood there in silence.
What should I have said?
Was there something I should do?
Before I could speak, a faint cry caught my attention. Jasmine clutched her head and began struggling with some kind of pain.
“Jasmine, what’s the matter!”
Before I could approach, Jasmine stuck her hand out signaling she was okay.
“Headaches. Extreme headaches.” She says as she adjusts herself. “They hurt, but it’s nothing I can’t handle.”
“Shouldn’t you go see a doctor? Those pains seem genuine.”
“It’s fine Luca, even if I did, I doubt they’d find anything.”
I was a bit puzzled. “You’re being awfully vague.”
“Snakes…” She takes a moment to explain, “Sometimes I see it, sometimes I don’t. But if I were to make out what it was, it was some kind of snake.”
“What are you talking about?”
Jasmine lifts her sleeves revealing a noticeable bite mark. Two holes marked the side of her biceps. I glared a bit closer, noticing no presence of blood or inflammation.
“I only see them through mirrors. This morning, I was able to sneak in a glimpse of something wrapped around my arm. Luca, what do you think?”
	“No doubt about it. This is clearly the work of an apparition.”
	Not only that, but I could also faintly make out a silhouette the harder I focused my eyes.
	“I thought so. This mark has been here for quite some time. I did get it checked out, but apparently no doctors noticed any bite marks. I thought I was going mad, but I’m a bit relieved to know it’s not in my head.”
	“I’ll have to meet with Kiku. If anything, it’s best to seek a professional for this.”
	“Kiku? Oh. That Japanese man.” She takes a moment to remember, “Kikuka Piero, it’s an unusual name, but if I recall, he helped you out with that spring incident, right?”
	“Yea, something like that.”
	“Interestingly, his first name ‘Piero’ is very uncommon. It’s made entirely of phonetic characters and quite literally translates to the name ‘Pierrot’. My best guess is that is his pseudonym.”
	“That is interesting. I’d have to ask him about that.”
	“His last name Kikuka is just means chrysanthemum flowers. Typically means longevity.”
	“Wow, you sure do know a lot about names.”
	“Is that so?” Jasmine drops her head. “Maybe that interest comes from not having agency in my own name.”
	“W-Well, I don’t think a name should define who you are as a person anyways. We’re always changing from the moment we are named.” I valiantly say.
	“Ooh! That’s some interesting insight.”
	“Huh? I-I was trying to cheer you up. But now I kind of feel made fun of…”
	“No no, I appreciate the gesture. Thanks.”
	Feeling a bit more encouraged, I turned to Jasmine to ask her something simple.
	“Let me help you.” I lower my head to show I truly meant it.
	She leans back from my abruptness. “Uhm, -.”
	“You said to not telling anyone about this morning. But the way I see it, that apparition thing is something separate. So long as I keep that promise, it’s all fair game, right?”
	She sighs but agrees in the end. “Okay then, please help me Luca.”
	“Before that,” I say while biting the surface of my thumb. I reach my arm towards her face.
	She looks at me uneased but accepts regardless. I smear her bruise with blood and her injury fades in seconds.
	“Sealing the contract with a blood print.” She excitedly says.
	“No weird remarks please.”
	“And now your soul is mine to keep.” She says with a deep voice
	“What kind of absurd contract is that! Scratch that, I want my soul back!”
	“Ahaha. It wouldn’t be the first time you sold your soul anyways.”
	With that exchange, I waved Jasmine goodbye. Since Kiku is a professional with these things, I’m sure he’d know what to do. In fact, he must have already known something befell her back in spring.
